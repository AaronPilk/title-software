# Tyler workflow addendum: commitments, CPLs and final policies

Research accessed September 11, 2026. Primary sources only. This addendum verifies the document and integration distinctions raised in Tyler’s recording. See the [complete call analysis](../discovery/tyler-call-analysis.md) for timestamped evidence and the [system blueprint](../system-blueprint.md) for implemented scope. The reported SoftPro Select/PTO/commitment/FTO process is company practice. Product decisions below are recommendations for a local synthetic prototype, not interpretations of a specific file or authorization to issue insurance.

## 1. Keep the documents and their schedules distinct

The ALTA 2021 commitment separates Schedule A, Schedule B Part I requirements, and Schedule B Part II exceptions. Requirements must be satisfied for the insurer’s issuance obligation; exceptions describe matters intended to remain outside the offered coverage unless cleared to the insurer’s satisfaction. The commitment is not the attorney’s title opinion. Its expiry interval is a form parameter: do not hardcode six months from explanations of older forms. [ALTA 2021 commitment redline, published July 30, 2021](https://www.alta.org/policies-and-standards/policy-forms/downloadSub?formSubID=2159&type=pdf).

**UI recommendation:** show separate lists for `Commitment requirements`, `Commitment exceptions` and `Final-policy exceptions`. Receipt of a final title opinion must not automatically clear every item. Use an evidence review transition: outstanding → evidence received → reviewed disposition. Preserve source wording, document version, item number and reviewer; retain an approved revision trail instead of deleting history. A final-policy comparison can propose “carry forward,” “revise,” or “omit,” each requiring a reason/reference and appropriate human review. These controls are design inferences from the distinction between the documents.

Do not interpret the label “B-II” without its document and policy context. In the 2021 loan-policy schedule, Part II can address subordinate matters and mortgage-priority coverage; it is not equivalent to commitment B-II. An owner policy’s Schedule B contains coverage exceptions. [Fidelity’s 2021 loan-policy comparison, pp. 36–37, updated July 14, 2021](https://media.fntic.com/ncs/2021policycomparisons/36-37/), [ALTA owner-policy redline, 2021](https://www.alta.org/policies-and-standards/policy-forms/downloadSub?formSubID=2154&type=pdf).

SoftPro already supports links between commitment items and policy exceptions/subordinate matters. Historical Select 4.3.31 release notes describe configurable linking through “Start Policy” and importing; linked edits can flow between screens. This is evidence for a future mapping requirement, not proof of how Tyler’s current configuration behaves. [SoftPro Select 4.3.31 FNF release notes, revised June 26, 2020, p. 9](https://fnfuserdocs.softprocorp.com/01fnfuserdocs/05ReleaseNotes/SoftPro_Select_Release_Notes_v4.3.31-FNF.pdf).

## 2. CPL is a separate product and a reviewed decision

“CPL only if a loan” should be retained as **Tyler’s reported routine**, not a universal legal rule or a prohibition on cash-file CPLs. The ALTA single-transaction CPL identifies a lender or a purchaser/lessee as potential protected addressee, subject to its requirements and exclusions. A CPL concerns specified closing-funds losses and is distinct from the title policy. [ALTA CPL single-transaction redline, adopted April 2, 2021](https://www.alta.org/policies-and-standards/policy-forms/downloadSub?formSubID=2135&type=pdf).

NC G.S. 58-26-1(a) permits specified closing-service insurance. Subsection (d) addresses pricing of combined title and closing-service insurance, including an undivided premium when both are issued. SC §38-75-1010 permits protection for a transaction party when a title policy will issue, limits covered conduct, and addresses approved premiums and nondivision of those premiums. Neither source supports a universal “no loan → CPL prohibited/unnecessary” rule. **Do not invent a shared flat CPL fee or apply the policy commission split to it.** [NC G.S. 58-26-1, current posted text; latest displayed amendment 2018](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_58/GS_58-26-1.html), [SC §38-75-1010, enacted June 11, 2012, current posted code](https://www.scstatehouse.gov/code/t38c075.php).

**UI recommendation:** a CPL card with decision `Review needed / Requested / Not requested—reason recorded`; covered party; requested recipient; underwriter; issuing agent/approved attorney reference; relevant loan; form version; provider reference; and separate prepared/issued/delivered/voided milestones. Default the decision prompt on financed transactions based on the recording, while keeping other reviewed outcomes possible. “Not requested” must not mean “legally not required.” In the local MVP, buttons create clearly labeled sample records or review summaries.

## 3. Model policy products, insured roles and forms separately

NC DOI distinguishes an owner’s policy protecting the owner from a lender’s policy protecting the lender’s security interest. The lender’s policy does not protect the homeowner. It also describes simultaneous issue pricing and separate owner/loan coverage amounts. [NC DOI, Title Insurance, undated current page](https://www.ncdoi.gov/consumers/homeowners-insurance/title-insurance).

**UI recommendation:** keep transaction type (`Purchase / Refinance / Other`) independent of policy selection (`Owner / Loan`, potentially both). Use separate owner/vesting and lender/proposed-insured fields, loan identifier, and policy amount. One order can contain multiple policy records; a lender recipient is not automatically the insured owner. Do not synthesize legal capacity or vesting text from a contact display name. Make each endorsement belong to a particular policy record, with requested-by, form code/version, purpose note, premium estimate, and review status.

ALTA’s active collection distinguishes base policies, short forms, expanded coverage, endorsements, commitments and CPL variants, and records effective/publication/technical-correction dates. The base policy’s label “2021” does not establish compatibility with every similarly labeled form. Form use/licensing and state-specific terms still need confirmation. [ALTA Policy Forms and Related Documents, current catalog](https://www.alta.org/policies-and-standards/policy-forms/).

**UI recommendation:** store form family, exact identifier/version, underwriter, property state, effective window and approval source. Seed a tiny illustrative catalog marked “availability unverified”; offer “Needs form selection” rather than automatically choosing legal coverage. Preserve the selected version on an existing file when catalog defaults change. An unsupported endorsement should block the simulated handoff rather than silently disappear.

## 4. Reuse verified SoftPro/underwriter workflow boundaries

The official agentTRAX guide distinguishes availability by company/underwriter/property state, CPL covered-party choices, owner/loan jackets, and final-policy image upload. It requires matching unmatched endorsements. It warns that some provider-screen edits do not write back to the SoftPro order, and that canceled CPLs require a new CPL rather than reinstatement. These are documented historical behaviors requiring current-account verification. [SoftPro agentTRAX guide, history updated August 2022, pp. 6, 14–19, 29–37](https://help.softprocorp.com/articles/360/AgentTRAX_UserGuide.pdf).

SoftPro’s current directory lists WFG title-search/evidence, CPL and policy-jacket integration. Thus local preparation should end in a reviewable handoff, with returned provider records tracked separately; existing integrations should be evaluated before implementing custom issuance. [SoftPro 360 Integrations, undated current directory](https://www.softprocorp.com/real-estate-software-solutions/softpro-360-data-integration/).

**Recommended demo flow:** PTO received → intake fields reviewed → commitment package prepared → CPL decision recorded → commitment/CPL handoff recorded → waiting for closing/FTO → final evidence and requirement dispositions reviewed → final policy package prepared → provider issuance recorded → final document/version and delivery recipients recorded. Allow holds and corrections within stages. Track preparation date, document effective date, issue date and delivery date independently.

NC law requires a qualifying attorney opinion and a separate insurer insurability determination. SC Bar guidance emphasizes review of the actual work, not blanket assurances; it is advisory guidance, not a software certification. The local UI should capture the attorney/underwriter evidence and responsible reviewer rather than infer legal sufficiency. [NC G.S. 58-26-1](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_58/GS_58-26-1.html), [SC Bar Ethics Advisory Opinion 09-01, 2009, currently published](https://www.scbar.org/for-lawyers/quicklinks/legal-resources/ethics-advisory-opinions/ethics-advisory-opinion-09-01/).
